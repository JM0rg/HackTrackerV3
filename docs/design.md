# Design

Player-scorer slowpitch. Our lineup only. Opponent = name + runs. No account required to score.

**You first.** Every install has a local Person. App opens on You. Teams are optional. The three tabs are You, Teams, and Games. Games brings every local team and personal game together, with live games first and filters for live, final, and personal. Account/settings opens More for appearance, backups, plan preview, and optional sign-in.

**Games.** Team: full lineup; the engine advances runners and the scorer corrects runs with a chip under the play. Personal: just you, in one of two scopes chosen on Log my game. *My At-Bats Only* keeps plate appearances and nothing else. *My At-Bats and Team Scores* also keeps both teams' scores: you enter all team runs independently of RBI, END HALF when our half ends by hand, and the opponent's half is the same card a team game uses; home or away decides who bats first and a line score falls out. Opponent and playing-with are optional. Playing-with has a Pick team button: choose a saved personal team to fill the name and tag the game, add a new one from the same sheet, or just type a name and save nothing. Personal teams are names only, per person, and separate from club Teams.

**The log.** A game is plate appearances plus finished opponent halves in one sequence. Score, outs, inning, bases and the batting order are all replayed from it, so a correction anywhere replays everything after it. Undo pops the newest entry of either kind; undoing an opponent half reopens its tally rather than dropping the runs.

**The journey.** Start, play, wrap, and no rooms in between. The You tab is home: a live game pinned on top with a Resume, one Start button that says what it will do, your numbers, and every game with your line on it. Starting is a sheet, not a screen: a mode segment and chips for team, home or away and opponent, pre-filled from last time; the opponent chip offers recent names before a keyboard. `/games/:id` is Field Mode while a game is live and the wrap once it is final, so ending or reopening re-renders in place. Closing Field Mode goes back to the tab and leaves the game live. Pull the score down and let go to end a game. The wrap leads with your line, keeps the score and line score if kept, lists the plays, and renders a share card. Team games start from the same sheet on the Team tab, with a lineup chip.

**Field Mode.** A framed scoreboard leads with US/THEM numerals, centered inning and out indicators, and a tappable situation line. A horizontal batter panel follows with jersey, name and the batting line; a personal game drops the name and shows YOUR DAY instead. The last play sits right above the diamond, in words ("Mike singled"), with its undo. The only scoring control is the batter's chip on home plate. Drag it to a base (1B/2B/3B), past the fence (HR), flick it down (OUT), The row under the diamond then asks what the drag left open, and nothing is filed until it is answered: an out asks Fly · Ground · Line · K, first base asks 1B · BB · ROE · FC, a personal hit asks RBI. A team double, triple or home run files on release. The chip is locked while a question stands, and the row carries a cancel; a run on a fly or line out is a sac fly, on a grounder an RBI groundout. Runners are chips that run when a play files; tap one to send them home. Swipe the name down to undo, sideways to move through the order. Tap the last-play line to fix it in words (error, forced runner, struck out, run scored) or change it outright. Tap the score line for the log. Their half is a full card: tap for a run, swipe up to end it. Dark scoreboard in both themes.

**Career.** You stats by `personId`. Filters (only if they play on a team): All, Free agent, per team.

**Durability.** Scoring commands and their rebuilt projections commit in one local transaction. Failed saves retain the play for Retry. Game settings are frozen at creation; correcting a final game preserves final status. Runs scored on later plays credit the runner who reached earlier. Walks cannot advance unforced runners through a run correction, and third-out batter/force plays cannot score runs.

**Handoff.** Team Field Mode has Handoff/Done. Handoff keeps the diamond and Undo available while hiding game administration and log editing. Passing the same phone requires no account or cloud connection.

**Personal scores.** In personal games that keep team scores, the half tally includes every team run. Recording RBI or marking your own run does not change that tally.

**Portable backup.** More has local export and restore. Backups include the local roster, game history, and identities, with a checksum for accidental corruption. They are not encrypted. Restore requires signing out and confirming replacement of local data; a failed restore rolls back. Schema 7 portable backups also restore into schema 8; nullable scoring fields are upgraded after checksum validation. Older unsupported database upgrades fail without erasing the source database.

**Cloud boundary.** App and launch-script configuration accept only the HackTracker Supabase endpoint. The server assigns a new team's creator as owner atomically; membership insertion and invitations have role ceilings. Full game sync remains unfinished.

**Runner corrections.** Each team PA can store a versioned list of runner destinations (out, first, second, third, scored), with RBI attribution separate from hit credit. The correction sheet supports multiple outs and explicit third-out timing. Replay validates runner identity and base occupancy; invalid dependent corrections roll back. Tapping a runner now changes that specific runner. The same sheet allows correcting the credited batter. New PAs freeze the batter gender used for coed walk rules. Unsupported result codes raise a recoverable interpretation error instead of becoming outs.

**Draft recovery.** Unanswered and failed plays persist in the local game's draft field. Drafts restore only when the batter and latest event sequence match. Writes are serialized; committing the PA clears its draft in the same transaction. Scoring and runner actions are locked while a write is pending. Drafts are included in portable backups but never sent to Supabase.

**Plan testing.** More includes a persisted Free / Player Plus / Team Plus preview in development builds. Every plan retains local scoring, teams, stats, and backups. Release builds ignore the preference and hide the switch. The preview models access and presents plan differences; it is not a server entitlement or a working cloud sync service. No store products or purchases are configured.

**Account linking.** Signing in links the local person without automatically uploading teams or claiming roles. Linking a scorebook already associated with another account is rejected and the new session is signed out. This guard protects the existing scorebook; full account-specific databases and cross-device reconciliation remain outstanding.

**Current verification.** Scoring, repository, draft recovery, backup upgrade, and plan-preview regression tests are automated. Golden snapshots load real icon fonts. Real-device performance profiling, complete cloud sync/entitlements, invite/claim client flows, missed-play checkpoints, durable redo, RSVP, and full screen-reader/large-text validation remain release work. These are not represented as completed by the plan preview.

**Field accessibility.** More offers persisted Outdoor contrast and Reduce motion settings. Outdoor contrast uses solid black with brighter labels and paths; the field painter updates when the palette changes. Diamond movements respect both the saved reduced-motion preference and the operating-system animation setting. Runners have 48-point touch heights, and base shortcuts have screen-reader labels. Games history builds cards lazily as they enter the viewport.

**Visual system.** The scorebook uses graphite surfaces, rounded panels, restrained emerald gradients, strong score numerals, and a compact floating tab dock. You opens with a personal scorebook illustration or career card. Teams opens with a clubhouse panel, record, live-game resume and management shortcuts. Games uses matchup cards with scores and status. Roster uses jersey tiles. The scoring diamond sits in its own tonal field panel. Light mode uses pale surfaces and a deeper green accent; Field Mode retains its dedicated dark palette. All artwork is code-native.

**Competitions and team stats.** Seasons and tournaments open a detail page with record, linked game log and scoped batting stats. Team stats use one reactive local query, filter by competition, exclude deleted games/plays, and use EXISTS to avoid counting multiply linked games twice. Batting cards show hits/AB, PA, 1B, 2B, 3B, HR, RBI, R, BB, K, SF, OBP, SLG and OPS. Players are listed by name, without a default ranking.

**Redesign verification.** Visual regression coverage includes team home, roster, game history, team stats, competition detail and Field Mode in both themes. These screens also run at double text size on a 390-point viewport; Field Mode scrolls when its controls need more space. Existing drag-scoring, undo, draft-recovery, handoff and final-game tests remain in the suite. A cancelled opponent-half swipe resets without ending the half, and its tally animation honors reduced motion.

**Contact scoring.** New-game sheets offer Track ball location, remembered for personal games and each team. Existing games keep base-drag scoring until the scorer explicitly changes the game menu setting; a pending draft blocks mode changes. Contact scoring uses a ball gesture followed by a result, never inferring hit credit from distance. BB/K shortcuts skip location, and dragging down asks the out type with unknown location. Personal scoring retains its explicit RBI question. Contact mode has a compact batter/score header and separate runner-to-home buttons, disabled while a play is pending. Tap placement and named area buttons provide alternatives to dragging. Contact type and batting side are optional; quality of contact remains opt-in.

**Full-field geometry.** The contact field is a true-scale schematic of 65-foot basepaths and a radial 300-foot fence. First/third are 65/300 of fence radius from home; second is 65√2/300. The fence joins the foul lines at ±45°. Thus second is about 31% of fence radius; the infield is not enlarged to change that relationship. Labels disclose the dimensions, which are a standard schematic rather than a measurement of the user's park. Ball markers and buttons have independent sizing. A drag aim point sits above the finger, and the region label updates separately. Crossing a fence or foul line does not determine scoring credit.

**Contact data.** Existing hit_location text stores legacy regions or version-1 JSON with x/y in fence-radius units, optional region, flight and frozen batting side. Unknown/legacy area observations never receive synthesized dots. The point is an estimate of first fielding/touch or untouched landing, not a measured flight distance. Points and result commit atomically, with version-2 drafts retaining location, question state and failed-save recovery. Corrections can edit or clear location without changing the outcome. BB/K writes clear location; changing the credited batter updates batting-side attribution. Portable backups preserve points and drafts without a local schema bump.

**Spray charts.** You, team stats, the game menu and box score link to local spray charts. Team/player, game and competition filters support personal and team scorebooks. Charts include located outs, show hits separately from other outcomes, and disclose location coverage against all batted balls (excluding BB/K). Area-only observations contribute to region counts but not plotted dots or depth estimates. Left/middle/right, shallow/mid/deep and pull/straight/opposite counts use the appropriate known-data denominators. Pull/opposite depends on recorded batting side; unknown switch-hitter sides are excluded. The log lists the latest 100 located plays while chart summaries include all matching observations.

**Production contact contract.** HackTracker uzyfohclwmkrqlfnlxxw has the contact_location_contract migration. A pure SECURITY INVOKER validator accepts existing region codes and bounded version-1 JSON, with a validated check constraint. Row-level security is retained. The remote table's pre-existing contact columns are reused; this does not complete the separate full-sync roadmap. SQL contract cases verify valid and malformed payloads. The existing authenticated join-team RPC advisory is unchanged.
